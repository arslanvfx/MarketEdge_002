KALSHI 15-MINUTE MARKET STABILITY ENGINE

Development Specification — Version 1.0

PURPOSE

Build a new, independent Market Stability Engine and Scheduler for Kalshi 15-minute markets.

This system is not primarily a directional prediction model.

Its main purpose is to answer:

“How likely is this specific 15-minute market window to remain sufficiently stable for our existing trading strategy?”

The engine should determine whether each upcoming/current 15-minute window is:

RUN — conditions are historically and currently stable enough.

CAUTION — conditions are borderline or uncertainty is elevated.

SKIP — conditions are unstable enough that the bot should not trade.

This system should run in parallel with the existing scheduler, not replace it initially.

The existing scheduler and the new Stability Engine must both log their decisions independently so their performance can be compared over a statistically meaningful sample.

⸻

1. CORE PHILOSOPHY

Do not attempt to determine whether BTC, ETH, XRP, DOGE, SOL, Gold, etc. will finish UP or DOWN unless directional information is useful as a secondary stability signal.

We care primarily about:

* volatility
* sudden acceleration
* price displacement
* reversal frequency
* market microstructure
* liquidity
* order-book behavior
* end-of-window instability
* abnormal market conditions
* cross-market stress
* scheduled economic events
* historical behavior of each specific 15-minute time slot
* current behavior relative to historical norms

The existing bot already has proximity logic determining how close the underlying asset is to the Kalshi target.

Do not remove that system.

This Stability Engine should become an additional risk filter above it.

Conceptually:

Existing Entry Logic
+
Existing Target Proximity Sensor
+
NEW Market Stability Engine
+
Risk / Scheduler Rules

Trade Permission

The stability system should be capable of saying:

NO TRADE

even if the existing proximity logic would otherwise permit an entry.

⸻

2. DO NOT HARD-CODE A “SAFE HOURS” SCHEDULE

Do not simply write rules such as:

2 AM–5 AM = SAFE

or:

9:30 AM–10:30 AM = BAD

Those observations may be useful as priors, but the system must determine stability empirically from collected data.

There is published evidence of meaningful intraday periodicity in cryptocurrency trading and volatility. Research using high-frequency BTC and ETH data has found recurring intraday liquidity/volatility patterns, and other research identifies activity peaks associated with major global trading sessions, including the U.S. market open.

However:

The application must independently calculate whether those patterns exist in the exact instruments we trade.

Historical assumptions are allowed.

Fabricated stability scores are not.

4. PRIMARY DATA SOURCE — CRITICAL

Crypto

The highest-priority underlying price feed should be:

Kalshi / CF Benchmarks Real-Time Index data.

Kalshi uses CF Benchmarks RTI pricing for crypto settlement.

The CF Benchmark index aggregates multiple major exchanges and updates once per second.

Kalshi provides CF Benchmark RTI data through its API.

Therefore:

Priority hierarchy

Priority 1

Kalshi CF Benchmarks RTI feed.

Priority 2

CF Benchmarks directly if appropriate access exists.

Priority 3

A multi-exchange composite constructed from major spot venues.

Priority 4

Individual highly liquid exchanges such as Coinbase/Kraken/etc. only as fallback/supporting sources.

Do not silently replace the Kalshi settlement feed with one exchange.

Record the data source used for every calculation.

5. DATA RESOLUTION

Fifteen-minute OHLC candles alone are NOT sufficient.

Preferred resolution:

1-second

Acceptable secondary resolution:

trade/tick-level

Fallback:

5-second

Absolute minimum for meaningful live stability analysis:

1-minute, but mark results as LOW DATA QUALITY.

Store 15-minute candles for summaries, but calculate stability from higher-resolution observations.

⸻

6. WHY 1-SECOND DATA MATTERS

The strategy operates near short-duration expirations.

A single 15-minute candle can hide an enormous amount of information.

Example:

Window opened:
$80,000

Window closed:
$80,010

A normal candle makes this look extremely stable.

But the actual path could have been:

80,000
→ 80,400
→ 79,600
→ 80,500
→ 80,010

That is not a stable window.

Therefore stability must be based on the complete price path, not merely open versus close.

10. NORMALIZED PRICE MOVEMENT

Do not compare assets using raw dollars.

A $100 BTC move is completely different from a $100 ETH move.

Calculate percentage or basis-point movement.

Use:

return_t = (price_t / price_previous) - 1

Also calculate basis points:

bps = return × 10,000

Normalize all relevant volatility measurements this way.

⸻

11. REALIZED VOLATILITY

Calculate realized volatility inside every 15-minute window.

At a minimum calculate:

RV = sqrt(sum(r_t²))

using high-frequency log returns.

Store:

* RV 15-minute
* RV last 5 minutes
* RV last 3 minutes
* RV final 60 seconds
* RV final 30 seconds

Then compare the current reading to that asset’s historical distribution for the same time-of-day.

Example:

BTC 14:30 ET current RV percentile:

92nd percentile

That should be treated much differently than:

34th percentile

⸻

12. RANGE VOLATILITY

range_pct = (high - low) / open

This captures total movement independent of final direction.

Also calculate rolling ranges:

* 15-minute
* 10-minute

* 3-minute
* 60-second
* 30-second

13. MAXIMUM SHORT-TERM SHOCK

Calculate the largest absolute movement occurring within:

* 1 second
* 5 seconds
* 10 seconds
* 30 seconds
* 60 seconds

max_abs_return_10s

14. VELOCITY

Calculate price velocity.

Example:

velocity_10s = abs(price_now - price_10s_ago) / price_10s_ago

Repeat for:

* 5 sec
* 10 sec
* 30 sec
* 60 sec
* 180 sec

15. ACCELERATION

Velocity alone is not enough.

We need to know whether movement is accelerating.

Example:

Previous 30-second movement:
3 bps

Current 30-second movement:
14 bps

This is more dangerous than a market continuously moving at 7 bps.

Create:

and:

Rapid increases should trigger defensive behavior.

16. REVERSAL / WHIPSAW DETECTION

This is extremely important.

A market that moves smoothly in one direction may actually be easier for the existing strategy to handle than a market repeatedly crossing back and forth.

Detect significant directional changes.

Possible methodology:

Define a meaningful price change threshold based on each asset’s volatility.

Count transitions such as:

UP
→ DOWN
→ UP
→ DOWN

within:

* 15 minutes
* final 5 minutes
* final 3 minutes
* final minute

Create:

reversal_count

reversal_rate

final_minute_reversal_count

whipsaw_score

Higher whipsaw = lower stability.

⸻

17. PATH EFFICIENCY RATIO

Calculate:

Efficiency Ratio = abs(close - open) / sum(abs(each incremental move))

Interpretation:

Near 1.0:

Price moved mostly smoothly in one direction.

Near 0:

Price traveled extensively but ended near where it began.

A low efficiency ratio indicates chop/whipsaw.

For this strategy that may be extremely important.

Store:

path_efficiency

⸻

18. TARGET CROSSING BEHAVIOR

Because the existing strategy already monitors distance to the Kalshi target, integrate that information rather than replacing it.

Calculate:

* number of target crossings
* target crossings final 5 minutes
* target crossings final 3 minutes
* target crossings final 60 seconds
* average duration above target
* average duration below target
* maximum distance from target
* minimum distance from target
* distance volatility
* crossing velocity

A market that crosses the target repeatedly should receive a substantial stability penalty.

Example:

Coin is only 0.05% from target but has crossed it eight times in three minutes.

That should be treated dramatically differently from a coin that is 0.05% from the target but has been drifting calmly away from it.

⸻

19. TARGET DISTANCE NORMALIZATION

Raw target distance is not enough.

Calculate:

normalized_target_distance = distance_to_target / expected_short_term_volatility

This answers:

How large is the target distance relative to what this asset normally moves during the remaining time?

Example:

A $50 distance in BTC might sometimes be meaningless.

A $50 distance in another asset could be enormous.

Normalize proximity against:

* asset volatility
* current volatility
* seconds remaining

33. DO NOT USE WIN RATE AS THE PRIMARY STABILITY VARIABLE

Trading win rate is useful, but it is contaminated by:

* entry strategy
* position timing
* target distance
* execution quality
* contract pricing
* strategy changes

Therefore calculate two independent quantities:

B. Strategy Performance

Do not confuse them.

A window can be stable but the strategy can trade it badly.

77. IMPORTANT: DO NOT OPTIMIZE ONLY FOR WIN RATE

Our actual economic objective should account for the asymmetric payoff structure.

A losing contract can lose nearly the entire position while a winning high-probability contract may earn only a relatively small percentage.

Therefore evaluate:

Expected Value

not merely:

Win Rate

Calculate:

EV = P(win) × average_net_win - P(loss) × average_net_loss

after fees and execution costs.

The Stability Engine is valuable only if it improves long-run risk-adjusted expected value.

FINAL DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLE

The purpose of this project is not to create a magical predictor.

We are building a quantitative risk detector.

The question is not:

“Will BTC go up or down?”

The question is:

“Does the current 15-minute environment statistically resemble the environments in which this strategy performs safely, or does it resemble environments associated with instability and losses?”

Every engineering decision should serve that question.

Build the dataset first.

Measure actual behavior.

Keep the existing model running.

Build the new model independently.

A/B test them.

Allow the evidence to determine which system is superior.

A couple of important points behind that spec: Kalshi explicitly says the CF Benchmarks RTI feed is available through its API and that crypto expiration uses the 60 one-second observations in the final minute, so I made final-minute behavior a major component instead of treating every minute in the 15-minute interval equally.  Published high-frequency research also supports your broader observation that crypto liquidity and volatility exhibit systematic intraday patterns rather than behaving uniformly throughout the 24-hour day. 

And there’s one addition in here that I think could make a big difference compared with the scheduler you already have: the deterioration/regime-change detector. Instead of a window slowly going from 85% → 80% → 75% → 65% while old wins keep propping up its historical rating, the new engine explicitly compares the last 7/14 days against the prior 30/60/90 days and can downgrade that window before the lifetime percentage collapses. That directly attacks the problem you were describing.
